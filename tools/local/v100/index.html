A “model” is billions of numbers

An AI model isn’t magic; it’s a giant table of weights, one number per learned connection. Qwen3-4B carries about 4.2 billion of them. Every answer you get is those numbers doing arithmetic on your input.

Speed is set by memory bandwidth

To write a single token, the model re-reads most of its weights from memory. That’s why a GPU with 936 GB/s streams tokens roughly ten times faster than DDR5 RAM at 96 GB/s, and why offloading layers to CPU RAM feels painfully slow.

The fork: local or cloud?

You can run these models on hardware you own; in Shenzhen, that’s about ¥0.65 per kWh of electricity, and your data never leaves the room. Or call a cloud API priced per million tokens. Both are legitimate; the right answer depends on your model, your usage, and what privacy means to you.

Local

  • Runs on your machine; no per-token billing
  • Cost = electricity (~¥0.65/kWh) + hardware you already own
  • Works offline; data stays with you

Cloud

  • Priced per million tokens (GPT-5, DeepSeek V4, Claude Opus 5)
  • No hardware to buy or maintain
  • Always the newest models, but your prompts leave the building
